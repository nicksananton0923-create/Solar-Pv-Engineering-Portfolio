# Safety Considerations — Solar PV Engineering Portfolio

## DC Voltage Hazard

PV strings generate DC voltage whenever illuminated. Even at low light, a 1000 V DC string can cause fatal electric shock.

- Use DC isolators on both ends of any string being worked on.
- Verify zero voltage with a calibrated DC voltmeter before any work.
- Cover PV modules with opaque material to stop generation during maintenance.
- Use DC-rated tools and PPE (arc-rated gloves, face shield).

## Arc Flash

DC arcs do not extinguish at zero crossing like AC arcs. A DC arc can sustain indefinitely and cause severe burns.

- Use arc-rated PPE for any DC work above 50 V.
- Use proper DC-rated disconnectors.
- Do not disconnect a string under load.

## High Temperature

PV modules can reach 70 °C+ in direct sun.

- Wear heat-resistant gloves.
- Schedule module-level work for early morning or late afternoon.

## Electrical Isolation

The inverter must be isolated on both DC and AC sides before any work.

- Apply LOTO on both DC and AC sides.
- Verify zero voltage on both sides.

## Earthing

PV systems require a dedicated earthing system per IEC 62548 / local code.

- Earth resistance must be measured and recorded.
- Equipotential bonding must be verified.

## Lightning Protection

PV arrays are exposed to lightning.

- SPDs (surge protection devices) are required per IEC 62305 / local code.
- Earthing system must be designed to dissipate lightning current.

## Working at Height

Ground-mounted systems are typically low but may require working at height on mounting structures.

- Follow working-at-height procedures.
- Use fall protection equipment.

## Disclaimer

This framework is documentation only. Engineering review by a competent solar PV engineer is required for any real project.
